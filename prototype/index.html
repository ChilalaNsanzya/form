Your carousel question — answered

Don't put a carousel in the hero slot

The horizontal gesture is already spoken for. If the whole page slides between Lifestyle → Shops → Homes, a hero that also swipes horizontally will eat those swipes and people will think navigation is broken. Two competing horizontal scrollers stacked at the top of a screen is the single most common super-app usability failure.

Keep the hero as one fixed, editorial slot — one merchant, one deal, one large image, one gold CTA. Rotate it server-side per session, not per swipe. Put the horizontal movement one level down in the second tier, where the cards are visibly narrower than the screen so the affordance reads as "this row scrolls", not "this page scrolls".

Why the layout changes per category

Lifestyle uses 2:3 posters — the format cinema, events and tourism content already ships in. Shops uses a 3-up logo grid because retailers are recognised by mark, not photo. Homes uses 2-up cards with price as the loudest element. Travel uses mode tiles, because people arrive knowing whether they need a flight or a bus. Services leads with search, because a directory with eight taxonomies cannot be browsed.

The Zanaco card is copper

One copper block per screen, appearing nowhere else in the app, so "this is Zanaco talking" is learnable in a single session. It is darker and deeper than the gold CTA, so the two warm tones separate by value rather than hue.

Each card sits after the marketplace content it belongs to — you see the house before you see the home loan, the shops before the grocery list.

Full-screen horizontal scroll — my read

Keep it, but as an accelerator

It is good at five pages and would be bad at seven. Two real problems to design around:

Nested rails eat the gesture. A swipe that starts on the poster rail or the tile grid scrolls that row, not the page. That is correct behaviour, but it means the page-swipe silently fails depending on where the thumb lands. Fix it by keeping a generous swipeable margin — the hero, the Zanaco card and the list are all full-bleed and non-scrolling, so most of the screen still pages.

Position memory. Each page must keep its own vertical scroll position when you swipe away and back, or returning to Shops from Homes dumps you at the top and the gesture feels lossy.

So: footer tap is the primary navigation and the thing you test with users; swipe is the accelerator for people who find it. Never make swipe the only way to reach anything.

One change to your footer order

You listed Lifestyle first; your screenshot has Shops first. Keep Shops in position 2 or 3 either way — it will carry the most traffic and the middle slots are the easiest thumb reach on a 6.7" phone. Five tabs is the ceiling; a sixth would force the labels below 10px.

Taking it into Figma

  1. Frame: iPhone 14 Pro, 393 × 852. Content gutter 16px. Bottom safe area 34px, so the tab bar floats 10px above it.
  2. Variables: create the token table in section 02 as a single collection named explore, modes light only for now.
  3. Components: build section 04 bottom-up — Row, then Card, then Rail, then Tab Bar. Every component gets auto-layout with vertical resizing set to hug.
  4. Screens: one frame per category, stacked left to right in the order of the footer. Set each frame's content to vertical scrolling.
  5. Prototype — footer: from each tab, Navigate to the matching frame, animation Smart animate, 250ms, ease out. Keep the tab bar as a fixed-position layer so it doesn't animate.
  6. Prototype — swipe: add On drag → Navigate to between adjacent frames in both directions, Move in / Move out, 250ms. This is what makes the whole-page slide work.
  7. Prototype — rails: set the second-tier row to horizontal scrolling and let the cards overflow the frame; Figma handles the rest.
  8. Zanaco cards: keep these as one component with a text/icon override per category, so a product change updates all five screens at once.
  9. Swipe memory: Figma will not preserve per-frame scroll position on drag-navigate. Note it as a build requirement for engineering rather than trying to fake it in the prototype.
